6 October,  15:00-16:00, STRAND BLDG S526

Speaker:&nbsp; **Claudia Pontuale** (Università degli Studi dell'Aquila)

**Title**:&nbsp; *Stability and Do Carmo's problem: from isotropic to anisotropic.*



**Abstract**:&nbsp; Do Carmo's problem asks whether every complete, noncompact, stable constant mean curvature (CMC) hypersurface in Euclidean space must be minimal. I will first review some classical and recent progress on this question, with particular emphasis on dimension-free rigidity phenomena and on their connection with the stable Bernstein problem. I will then turn to the anisotropic counterpart of Do Carmo's question, where stability can still impose strong restrictions on the geometry at infinity. In particular, I will present a one-endedness theorem for stable constant anisotropic mean curvature hypersurfaces under a natural ellipticity condition and discuss the main ideas behind its proof.

13 October,  15:00-16:00, STRAND BLDG S526

Speaker:&nbsp; **Stephen Lynch**  (KCL)

**Title**:&nbsp; Surgery for the mean curvature flow in higher codimension

**Abstract**:&nbsp;  The mean curvature flow offers a powerful mechanism for evolving extrinsic geometries into canonical forms. However solutions are subject to finite-time singularity formation. A profound problem, with many potential applications, is to reveal the geometric information encoded in these singularities. I will discuss work with Huy Nguyen in which we construct a flow with surgery for submanifolds of higher codimension under a natural curvature pinching condition. The key new contribution is fine control on singularities, allowing for them to be removed, after which the flow can continue smoothly. The construction has topological consequences.

20 October,  15:00-16:00, STRAND BLDG S526

Speaker:&nbsp; **Ivan Smith** (Cambridge)

**Title**:&nbsp; Floer homotopy and bordism



**Abstract**:&nbsp; Floer homotopy theory aims to extract information from moduli spaces of holomorphic polygons with boundaries on Lagrangian submanifolds, whereas classical Floer homology largely focusses attention on counting zero-dimensional spaces of such polygons. We discuss constraints on the bordism classes of Lagrangians that arise from this theory, focussing attention on the case of exact symplectic manifolds (e.g. smooth complex affine varieties). This talk reports on joint work with Noah Porcelli.

27 October,  15:00-16:00, STRAND BLDG S526

Speaker:&nbsp; **Simon Salamon** (KCL)

**Title**:&nbsp; Counting invariant spinors on unitary flag manifolds



**Abstract**:&nbsp; Using the concept of balanced subgraph, we show that the dimension of the space of invariant spinors on U(n)/T^n (with n necessarily odd) is equal to the number of regular tournaments with n nodes (for example, 2640 for n=7). The associated representation theory leads to an analysis of the Dirac operator. This is joint work with Diego Conti, and the problem arose from work of Diego Artacho, Marie-Amelie Lawn, and Uwe Semmelmann.

3 November,  15:00-16:00, STRAND BLDG S526

Speaker:&nbsp; **Albert Wood** (KCL)

**Title**:&nbsp; Lagrangian Mean Curvature Flow with Symmetries in Calabi-Yau Manifolds




**Abstract**:&nbsp; Lagrangian mean curvature flow (LMCF) is the name given to the fact that the mean curvature flow of submanifolds preserves the class of Lagrangian submanifolds in a Calabi-Yau manifold. Since special Lagrangian submanifolds are volume-minimising in their homology class, LMCF has been proposed as a technique to prove existence of special Lagrangian submanifolds in suitable classes of Lagrangians. However, finite-time singularities occur in general along the flow, and understanding these singularities is therefore of great importance.

In this talk I will discuss upcoming joint work with Dr. Ben Lambert, in which we completely classify singularities of LMCF in very general cohomogeneity-one symmetric settings. I will cover the basics of the subject as well as concrete examples of LMCFs in symmetric Calabi-Yau manifolds to which our theory may be applied, before giving an overview of the key steps of our proof and a statement of the general result.

10 November,  15:00-16:00, STRAND BLDG S526

Speaker:&nbsp; **Jonny Evans** (Lancaster)

**Title**:&nbsp; Weighted Seshadri constants and ellipsoid embeddings



**Abstract**:&nbsp; Quantitative symplectic geometry studies questions like "How big a ball can one embed symplectically into a given symplectic manifold?" If one deforms the ball into an ellipsoid then the answer to the corresponding ellipsoid embedding problem is very sensitive to the precise shape of the ellipsoid. In 2009, McDuff and Schlenk discovered an intricate infinite staircase pattern which encoded the answer to this question for embedding ellipsoids into the complex projective plane: there were infinitely many steps whose combinatorics was encoded by Fibonacci numbers, which we now understand quite well, and then nine exceptional "post-Fibonacci" steps, which are more mysterious. Exactly the same staircase appeared five years later in a paper of Dumnicki, Harbourne, Kuronya, Roe and Szemberg on the Nagata conjecture for plane curves with prescribed valuative behaviour near a point. I will explain how the algebro-geometric perspective helps us to understand the post-Fibonacci steps.
